01 Scope Sensitivity Matcher

Matches actual screen turn-rate (pixel speed) across scopes, since equal menu sensitivity values do not produce equal turn speed — each scope has its own A/B constants. Set the scope you like, and get matched values for every other scope.

OPTIC · PUBG ARTWORK1x Red DotPUBG / KRAFTON artwork. Scope conversion uses the existing sensitivity maths.

Find a setting you like, then match the others

  1. In PUBG, choose an aiming mode or scope that feels comfortable. Enter that mode and its current sensitivity below.
  2. The reference speed is calculated in px/s. Copy the matched sensitivities into your game settings to give the other scopes the same calculated speed.
  3. Or enter all your current settings in the speed checker below. See which aiming modes are fastest and slowest, and how large the mismatch is.
  4. Test your changes in Training Mode. Use the vertical multiplier section below if you also change your preferred ADS sensitivity; use the resolution section when changing aspect ratio.

Visual spray simulator

Each Fire rolls a new random spray with the selected stance and attachments. The first bullet starts at the crosshair; recoil moves the following bullet. These are simulated positions in curve units, not measured screen pixels or guaranteed impacts. Five colours let you compare independent sprays.

0 / 5 sprays

The view keeps the same scale when attachments or stance change. After five sprays, Fire starts a new set. Changing the setup clears the previous set.

Every shot’s curve values

Curves are sampled at the selected firing interval with their recorded linear, constant or cubic interpolation. The 10-shot windows are a display choice, not a claim that PUBG changes mechanics at shot 10 or 20.

How PUBG damage works

01 / THE FORMULA

Damage Calculation

Actual damage is base damage multiplied by four factors: distance, armour remaining, hit area and weapon class. This worked example follows your weapon, gear and selected body zone in Combat Lab.

Actual damage

02 / WHERE THE BULLET LANDS

Hit Area Modifier

Each of the thirteen precise body regions has its own hit-area factor. These factors stay the same across the listed classes; class and gear modifiers are applied separately. Left and right limbs share the same values.

100% means ×1.00; 110% means ×1.10. This layer alone does not represent final damage. The neutral training mannequin uses the same anatomical regions as the interactive map. Leader lines are diagram references, rather than an exact PUBG collision mesh.

03 / WHAT YOU FIRE

Weapon Class Modifier

Compare all seven class profiles. SMGs give more weight to limb hits; sniper rifles give more weight to head and torso hits. These models show only the class factor, before hit-area and gear effects.

Class defaults are shown here. Weapon-specific exceptions still apply in the live calculation: Dragunov uses ×2.80 for head and neck instead of the DMR default ×2.35. LMG pelvis and torso factors differ.

04 / THE COMPLETE MULTIPLIER

Combined Multipliers: Weapon Class, Hit Area & Gear

Class × hit area × armour remaining. Change the helmet and vest to compare protection across every class. Distance is excluded from this view.

The guide exposes the same multiplier data used by our damage engine. PUBGStatistics reference. Armour factors assume intact standard gear: Level 1 ×0.70, Level 2 ×0.60, Level 3 ×0.45. Helmets apply to head/neck, vests to torso/pelvis, and limbs stay unprotected. Class diagrams show class defaults rather than every weapon exception. Shotgun multipliers are per-pellet factors, not guaranteed whole-shell damage.
